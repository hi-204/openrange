Poker has written rules and unwritten ones. Following both keeps games friendly and keeps you
out of disputes that can cost you a pot.

## Act in turn

Wait until it is your turn. Acting early (folding, or reaching for chips) gives information to
players before you and is unfair to them. At most tables an action taken out of turn can be
binding.

## Make actions clear

- **Say it.** Saying "call", "raise to 25" or "fold" is binding at most live tables, and it
  avoids confusion.
- **One motion with chips.** Put your chips forward in one go. Bringing chips out in several
  trips without announcing first is a [[string bet]], and the dealer may count only your first
  amount.
- **Don't throw chips into the pot.** Place them in front of you so the bet can be counted.

## Protect your hand

Keep your cards on the table, in front of you, ideally with a chip on top. If a dealer or
another player's discard touches your unprotected cards, your hand may be declared dead. Don't
[[muck]] (throw away) a hand that might win until the pot is awarded.

## Be a good opponent

- Don't comment on a hand you're not in, and don't show your cards to one player only.
- Don't slow-roll (take ages to show the winning hand at showdown).
- Avoid [[angle shooting]]: moves that are technically allowed but meant to mislead, such as
  pretending to fold or hiding chips.

## Online

- Use the time you need, but don't stall on purpose.
- Keep the chat friendly or leave it off.
- **Fair play:** most poker sites forbid any software that advises you during a hand. OpenRange
  is a study tool for use away from the table. It has no live-play features by design.

## Worked hand

You are in the cutoff at a live table. The player before you is still thinking. You've decided
to fold and want to hurry things along.

1. **Wait.** Folding before your turn tells the player before you that one fewer opponent is
   left, which can change their decision.
2. When it's your turn, push your cards face down toward the dealer.
3. Later you have a strong hand and want to raise to 20. Say "raise to twenty" and then put
   the chips out, so nobody can argue that you only called.

## Common mistakes

- **String betting** by accident: say the amount first.
- **Leaving cards unprotected** while you talk or look at your phone.
- **Mucking too early** at showdown when you might have the best hand.
