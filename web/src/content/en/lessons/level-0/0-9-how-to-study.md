You learn poker the way you learn a language: a little every day, using what you learn as
soon as you learn it. This lesson is a plan for using OpenRange that way.

## A session in four steps

A good session fits in a coffee break:

1. **Warm up** with a few Math Lab problems (the menu's Math Lab): pot odds, outs, combos.
2. **Learn** one lesson, start to finish.
3. **Practise** it at once: every lesson ends with "Practice this", a drill on the same idea.
4. **Review** what the trainer brings back: the review queue on Home's daily plan.

The daily plan on Home suggests these steps for you. Skip any of them; nothing is lost.

## Why the review queue matters

People forget most new things within days unless they meet them again.
[[Spaced repetition]] fights that: a spot you answer well comes back after a longer gap, and a spot you
miss comes back soon. OpenRange's trainer schedules every spot family this way, so a short
review session each day keeps old lessons fresh.

```callout key
Practise a little every day rather than a lot once a week: the gaps between sessions are part
of how memory works.
```

## Practise on purpose

[[Deliberate practice]] means working on what you're worst at, not what you already enjoy:

- **Stats → Practice my weak spots** builds a session from the spots you get wrong.
- **Challenge sets** in the Math Lab give you a short, focused round with a summary.
- **Equity estimation** in the Math Lab tracks whether your guesses run high or low.

## Measure the right thing

Poker results are noisy: you can play well and lose, or badly and win. Your decisions are what
you control. That's why OpenRange grades every answer by how much expected value it gives up,
never by whether the hand won.

## Worked hand

You play a session against the bots (Play → Sessions) and lose a big pot with top pair.

1. **Review** the hand straight after it ends: "Review this hand" shows each of your decisions
   with its reference, where one exists.
2. **Write a note** on the spot ("Your notes on this spot") so the lesson isn't lost.
3. **Drill** that kind of spot in Train, and let the review queue bring it back.
4. **Reflect** briefly in the journal (lesson 6.10) about how you played, not how you ran.

## Common mistakes

- **Long cramming sessions.** Short, regular sessions stick far better.
- **Reading without drilling.** Knowledge becomes skill only when you use it.
- **Chasing results.** A won pot doesn't prove the play was right.
- **Skipping review.** Old spots fade unless they come back.
