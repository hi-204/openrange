[[Tilt]] is playing worse than you know how to because you're upset, frustrated or bored.
Variance (lesson 6.5) guarantees bad runs, so every player meets it.

## Signs of tilt

- You play hands you'd normally fold, to "win it back".
- You call because you "can't believe" they have it.
- You keep playing longer than you planned, or faster than usual.
- You replay a bad beat in your head during the next hands.

## A routine that works

Decisions made before a session are calmer than decisions made during one:

- **A [[stop-loss]]:** decide in advance to stop after losing a set amount, say a few buy-ins.
- **Breaks:** stand up after a big pot, win or lose.
- **A short check-in:** before each session, ask whether you're rested and focused.
- **Review later, not now:** note the hand and look at it after the session.

```callout key
Results are noisy; decisions are what you control. A good decision that lost is still a good
decision.
```

## Decisions, not results

After a loss, ask: was my decision right given what I knew? OpenRange's trainer grades
decisions by EV, never by whether the hand won, for the same reason. If a good decision lost,
nothing needs fixing.

## Worked hand

You get all-in with A-A against K-K before the flop and lose to a king on the river. Two hands
later you hold Q-9 offsuit under the gun and feel like playing it.

1. **Name it:** you want to win the money back; that's tilt talking.
2. **Check the plan:** Q-9 offsuit isn't in the UTG range (lesson 1.5).
3. **Fold, and take a break** at the end of the orbit.

## Common mistakes

- **Thinking you're immune.** Everyone tilts in some way.
- **Making stop rules during the session.** Set them before.
- **Judging the A-A hand by the result.** It was a great all-in.
