The hands you play are your best study material, if you can find them again and look at them
calmly. Notes and a [[session review]] make that possible.

## Useful notes

A useful note says what happened, in what spot, and how often you've seen it:

- **Vague:** "player 4 is aggressive".
- **Useful:** "3-bet the button twice in an orbit from the big blind; showed down a weak ace
  once".

Write facts, the spot and the sample size. Opinions without context age badly.

## A short review routine

1. **Pick three to five hands** where you weren't sure, not just the big losses.
2. **Rebuild the spot** in OpenRange: the chart for the preflop decision, a solve for a river.
3. **Write one lesson** you'll apply next time.
4. **Drill it:** the Train pages and the review queue bring spots back.

```callout key
Study after you play, never during. Most online rooms forbid real-time assistance, and
OpenRange is built only for study: it has no live overlay and never reads a poker client.
```

## Worked hand

After a session you remember folding top pair to a river overbet and wondering if it was right.

1. **Note:** the board, the sizes, and what you know about the player.
2. **Rebuild:** a river spot with similar ranges and an overbet size in the solver.
3. **Lesson:** against a polarized overbet, top pair is a bluff catcher (lessons 4.8 and 5.2);
   the price and the opponent's bluffing frequency decide.

## Common mistakes

- **Reviewing only big losses.** Close decisions teach more.
- **Using study tools while playing.** It's against most sites' rules and it isn't learning.
- **Writing notes without context.** Without the spot, a note can mislead you later.
