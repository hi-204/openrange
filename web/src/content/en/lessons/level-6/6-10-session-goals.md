Lesson 6.7 was about noticing [[tilt]]. This lesson adds the routine around a session: a goal
before you play and a short reflection after.

## Goals you control

A result goal ("win today") depends on the cards. A [[process goal]] depends only on you:

- "Fold the bottom of my opening range under the gun."
- "Take a break after every big pot."
- "Stop when I notice I'm replaying a bad beat."

You can meet a process goal on a losing day, and miss it on a winning one. That's the point: it
measures the part you control.

## Before you play

A check-in takes a minute:

- **Rested and focused?** If not, play less, or study instead.
- **Stop rule set?** A stop-loss and a time limit, decided now (lesson 6.7).
- **One goal.** Pick a single process goal; more than one gets forgotten.

## After you play

Answer a few questions while the session is fresh:

- **How did you feel,** and were there moments of tilt?
- **What went well?** Name a decision you're happy with.
- **What would you change?** Name a decision to review.
- **One focus for next time.** Turn the answer into your next process goal.

```callout key
Write it down. A reflection you only think about fades by the next session.
```

OpenRange keeps these in a private journal (Play → Journal), stored only in this browser and
included in your backup.

## Worked hand

Your goal for the session: "take a break after every big pot". You win a big pot, skip the
break, and lose two quick hands playing faster than usual.

1. **Check-in after:** you felt rushed and a little tilted after the pot.
2. **Went well:** you folded a marginal hand early in the session that you'd usually call.
3. **To change:** the two quick hands; review them in the hand review.
4. **Next focus:** "take a break after every big pot", again, because you missed it.

## Common mistakes

- **Result goals.** They make good sessions feel bad and bad ones feel good.
- **Too many goals.** One is enough.
- **Reflecting only after losing sessions.** Winning sessions hide mistakes too.
