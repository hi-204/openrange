Solvers can produce more numbers than anyone can learn. Good study turns a few solves into a
few rules, and practises them until they're automatic.

## Pick the spots

Start with the spots you meet most often:

- single-raised pots, button against the big blind;
- the blinds against each other;
- 3-bet pots in position.

Rare spots matter less, however interesting they are.

## A weekly routine

1. **One spot a session.** Solve it, read it in the order of lesson 7.1.
2. **One or two rules.** For example, "medium hands bet small on dry rivers".
3. **Drill it.** The Train pages turn spots into questions, and the review queue brings back
   what you miss (spaced repetition).
4. **Check your play.** In a [[session review]] (lesson 6.8), look for hands where the rule
   applied.

```callout key
A rule you use beats a hundred numbers you remember for a day.
```

## What OpenRange does for you

- The preflop and postflop drills grade each decision by EV, and mixes are never marked wrong.
- The Stats page groups your answers and waits for enough of them before showing a number.
- The review queue schedules weak spots to come back sooner.

Aggregated reports over many flops are planned for a later version.

## Worked hand

You keep losing points on river decisions with medium hands.

1. **Solve** the river spot of lesson 4.9 and read it.
2. **Rule:** medium hands bet small or check; big bets are polarized.
3. **Drill** that spot and let the review queue bring it back over the next days.

## Common mistakes

- **Studying rare spots first.** Common spots pay more.
- **Memorising frequencies.** Learn the rule behind them.
- **Studying without practising.** Drills turn knowledge into habit.
